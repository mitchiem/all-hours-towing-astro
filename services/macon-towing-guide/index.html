Georgia Towing Laws Every Macon Driver Should Know

The Georgia Move Over Law (O.C.G.A. 40-6-16) requires drivers to move over one lane when approaching a stationary vehicle with flashing lights, including tow trucks, and to slow down when a lane change is unsafe. This keeps operators safe on I-75, I-16, I-475, and busy Macon streets.

For nonconsensual towing from private property, Georgia law sets maximum fees and requires proper signage before a vehicle can be removed. Tow companies must follow state procedures for notification, billing, and storage. When you are towed, you have the right to an itemized statement of charges.

You also have the right to choose your towing company after most accidents and breakdowns unless law enforcement specifically directs the tow. You are never required to use a company simply because it arrived first or was called by a third party.

Safety first. Signal, ease to the shoulder or a parking lot, and turn on hazard lights. On I-75 or I-16, stay in the vehicle with your seatbelt fastened while waiting if traffic is moving; standing near fast traffic is more dangerous. Note the nearest exit number or mile marker.

What to Do If Your Car Was Towed

First, find where it went. Ask the property owner, nearby business, or local police; most nonconsensual tows are logged with law enforcement. Collect your driver's license, registration, and proof of insurance before going to the lot.

Ask for a written, itemized list of fees and compare against Georgia's maximums for nonconsensual tows. Retrieve the vehicle as soon as possible because daily storage fees accumulate. Inspect the vehicle for new damage before leaving, photograph anything unusual, and keep all receipts for insurance or a legal dispute.
